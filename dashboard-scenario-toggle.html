Priority 5 Control
Integration Step Drop-off
Integration Step Drop-off (Step 5 — Connect Calendar/Tool): Agents reaching the integration/permission step frequently abandon onboarding entirely here, since they aren't ready to grant that access yet. This is the largest single driver of mid-onboarding abandonment in Control.
Suggestion
Make the integration step skippable, with a clear "connect later" path that doesn't block onboarding completion.

Priority 3 Control
Step Fatigue
Step Fatigue (Steps 4-6): By the later screens, agents stop engaging carefully and click through without reading, reducing both answer quality and reported confidence in what they agreed to.
Suggestion
Cut or consolidate the later steps — this is the core justification for testing a shorter flow.

Step 4 — Goal selection
Priority 3 Control
No Sense of Progress
No Sense of Progress (Steps 1-6): Without a visible step count, agents can't tell how much of the flow remains, which several described as a reason they considered abandoning partway through.
Suggestion
Add a persistent step indicator (e.g., "Step 3 of 6") across the whole flow.

Priority 2 Control
Duplicate Information Request
Duplicate Information Request (Step 3 — Team Size): Onboarding re-asks for information already provided at signup, which agents noticed and flagged as mildly frustrating rather than flow-ending.
Suggestion
Pre-fill fields already captured at signup instead of re-asking during onboarding.

Step 3 — Team size
Priority 4 Variant
Missing Post-Onboarding Context
Missing Post-Onboarding Context (Step 3 — Review → App): Agents finish quickly but land in the product without knowing where key features live, since explanatory content that existed in Control's longer flow was cut. This is the direct driver of the Confusion guardrail breach.
Suggestion
Add a brief, dismissible in-app guide covering the 2-3 things onboarding used to explain, rather than cutting that content entirely.

Step 3 — Entering app post-onboarding
Priority 4 Variant
Rushed Personalization
Rushed Personalization (Step 2 — Combined Details): Role, team size, and goal are combined into a single dense screen with no explanation of why each matters, leading agents to default to the fastest answer. This is the direct driver of the Setup Completeness guardrail breach.
Suggestion
Keep the screen count low, but add brief inline context for why each field matters rather than presenting them as a bare form.

Step 2 — Combined details screen
Priority 3 Variant
Unclear Product Value
Unclear Product Value (Steps 1-3): The shorter flow completes fast but doesn't leave agents with a clear sense of why the product matters, which several linked to lower early motivation even when they chose to continue.
Suggestion
Use one of the three steps to briefly frame the core value proposition, rather than only collecting setup information.

Visual Hierarchy & Contrast
Visual Hierarchy & Contrast (Step 1-2 — Product Page → Cart): The blue CTA reads close to the surrounding UI chrome. Several agents scrolled past it on first pass and had to backtrack to locate it.
Suggestion
Increase contrast between the CTA and its background — this is the issue the Variant's color change was designed to address.

Priority 2 Variant
Interaction & Layout
Interaction & Layout (Step 3 — Checkout): On mobile, several agents described the checkout button as difficult to tap precisely, independent of its color. The touch target didn't change between variants, so this persists here too.
Suggestion
Increase the CTA's tap target to at least 44×44px with additional padding, regardless of which color ships.

Positive Variant
Visual Hierarchy & Contrast
Visual Hierarchy & Contrast (Step 2 — Cart): Agents in the Variant group located and reached the checkout CTA faster and more directly than in Control, several noting the higher-contrast green stood out against the page.
Why this worked
Higher contrast against the surrounding page reduced time spent visually searching for the next step — keep this contrast level in future iterations.
